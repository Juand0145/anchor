You identify HIPAA Administrative Simplification requirements inside the chunk
(45 CFR Parts 160, 162, 164 — Privacy, Security, Breach Notification, and
General/Administrative provisions) so their boundaries can be anchored.

These rules decide ONLY which spans are requirements and how requirement_id is
formatted. The output shape (anchors and status) is defined by the anchor
instructions above.

REQUIREMENT IDENTIFIER (requirement_id):
- CFR-style hierarchical id WITHOUT the leading "§", e.g. "164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A)",
  "164.306(a)(1)", "164.310(b)", "160.102". Concatenate the section number with
  the parenthetical hierarchy where the obligation appears.
- Heading anchoring: emit a requirement for section "NNN.NNN" only if the literal
  heading "§ NNN.NNN" (allow spacing artifacts like "§NNN.NNN") appears in THIS
  chunk. If the governing heading is in a previous chunk, do not emit it here.
- No subparagraph promotion: (a) (b) (1) (2) (i) (ii) always belong to the nearest
  preceding "§ NNN.NNN" heading; never promote a subparagraph to a sibling
  section number.
- No section interpolation: HIPAA sections are not consecutive; never invent a
  section to fill a numeric gap.
- Use the letters and roman numerals exactly as printed; never re-letter,
  re-number, or normalize.

WHAT IS A REQUIREMENT (emit these):
- Each "Standard: <name>." paragraph that states an obligation.
- Each implementation specification marked "(Required)" or "(Addressable)". When a
  Standard is followed by lettered specs (A) (B) (C)..., the Standard AND each spec
  are separate requirements.
- Numbered/lettered paragraphs containing an obligation verb (must, shall,
  implement, establish, ensure, conduct, identify, apply, document, maintain,
  perform, review, report).
- Substantive Privacy Rule (Part 164 Subpart E) and Breach Notification Rule
  (Part 164 Subpart D) obligations, and organizational requirements (§ 164.105,
  § 164.314, § 164.504, § 164.530).
- Compliance/investigation obligations on covered entities or business associates
  in Part 160 Subpart C (e.g. § 160.310, § 160.316).

GRANULARITY AND BOUNDARIES:
- Applicability/scope/enumeration sections that merely LIST regulated entities,
  persons, or transactions are ONE requirement for the WHOLE section (id = the
  section number, e.g. "160.102"). The start_anchor is the "§ NNN.NNN <title>"
  heading line; the end_anchor is the LAST text of the section — normally the
  trailing regulatory-history bracket "[NN FR ..., ... 20NN]", otherwise the final
  clause immediately before the next "§ NNN.NNN" heading.
  Capture the ENTIRE section: ALL lettered/numbered paragraphs (a) (b) (c) ... and
  their nested items, not just the first list. Do NOT stop at the last item of the
  first "(a)" enumeration, and do NOT split "(a) (1) (2) (3) (b) (c)" into separate
  requirements — a bare item like "A health plan." is meaningless on its own.
  Example: for "§ 160.102 Applicability." the single requirement "160.102" runs
  start_anchor "§ 160.102 Applicability." -> end_anchor "78 FR 5687, Jan. 25,
  2013]" (the section's trailing history bracket), spanning (a) (1) (2) (3) (b) (c),
  NOT ending at "(3) ... transaction covered by this subchapter.".
- Contrast: a Standard with implementation specifications is the opposite case —
  the Standard AND each "(Required)" / "(Addressable)" spec are SEPARATE
  requirements. When a Standard or spec has its own visible terminal sentence,
  clause, or paragraph before the next identifier, emit an end_anchor for that
  terminal text. Use null end_anchor only when the next "(Required)",
  "(Addressable)", numbered/lettered identifier, or "§ NNN.NNN" heading starts
  immediately after the current requirement's final punctuation with no
  intervening non-requirement prose.
